Free Chinese Resources for International Students

China makes a lot of genuinely free things that international students can use: AI chat tools, Chinese-learning apps, open university courses, a classical-texts library, and even a full scholarship to study in China. This page lists only official websites. There is nothing to download from this page, and most of these work without a Chinese phone number.

Read this first: free chat is not the same as a free API. The chat websites below are free for personal use; the developer APIs behind them are usually paid by usage. Also, do not paste passwords, personal documents, or confidential work into any free AI chat — these services store your chats on their own servers.

1. Free AI chat from China

DeepSeek — chat.deepseek.com. A Chinese AI lab's free chat, on the web and in the official iOS and Android apps. Good for explaining a concept, drafting an essay outline, or debugging code. Sign in with an email, Google account or Apple ID. The chat itself is free with no published daily message limit; only the developer API is billed by usage. DeepSeek says data is stored on servers in China, so keep sensitive material out of it.

Kimi — kimi.ai (international) / kimi.com (mainland China). Moonshot AI's assistant, strong on long documents, deep research, and turning one prompt into slides, documents or code. The consumer chat is free with a daily quota; paid plans exist for heavier use. Use the international site kimi.ai if you are outside China — it accepts a phone number or a Google account. The mainland site kimi.com accepts WeChat or a mainland-China phone number, which most international students do not have.

Qwen — chat.qwen.ai. Alibaba's AI assistant, also known as Tongyi Qianwen . Free chat on the web and in the official apps, for writing, documents, images, and web-assisted research. Sign in with an email, Google, Apple or GitHub account. The chat is free for personal, non-commercial use; the API on Alibaba Cloud is paid.

Tip: use all three for a week and keep the one that answers your kind of questions best. They are strongest in slightly different places — DeepSeek for reasoning and code, Kimi for long documents and research, Qwen for general chat and multimodal tasks.

2. Learn Chinese for free

HelloChinese. A beginner-friendly app that teaches pinyin, characters, speaking and listening in short lessons. The free tier covers the core lessons; it is in the App Store and Google Play — search for the official "HelloChinese" app.

Duolingo — Chinese course. If you already use Duolingo, add Chinese as a language. The free tier includes the full course with ads; it is best for daily vocabulary and character recognition, not for speaking practice on its own.

Chinese Text Project — ctext.org. An open-access digital library of premodern Chinese texts, run since 2006. Over 30,000 titles, from the Analects and Daoist classics to the dynastic histories, with Chinese-English parallel texts aligned paragraph by paragraph, plus a built-in dictionary. Free to read and search in a browser, no account needed; the interface is in English and Chinese, and you can switch between traditional and simplified characters with one click. Good for students of Chinese history, philosophy, or classical Chinese.

3. Free university courses from China

China University MOOC — icourse163.org. China's national MOOC platform, with courses from Peking University, Tsinghua, Zhejiang University and many others. Most courses are free to audit and watch; a certificate, where offered, may be paid. Many courses are in Chinese, and some offer English subtitles — check the course page before enrolling. Useful if you want a free introduction to a subject from a Chinese university, or to practise listening to academic Chinese.

4. Study in China for free: the CSC Scholarship

Chinese Government Scholarship (CSC) — campuschina.org. Run by the China Scholarship Council for the Ministry of Education, this funds international students to study at Chinese universities — undergraduate, master's, doctoral, Chinese-language, and visiting-scholar programmes.

  • A full scholarship waives tuition and registration fees, gives free on-campus dormitory accommodation, a monthly living allowance, a one-off settlement subsidy, and medical insurance. A partial scholarship covers some of these items. The exact allowance depends on your level and programme, so check the official page for the current year.
  • You must be a citizen of a country other than China and in good health. Most programmes are taught in Chinese; some graduate programmes are taught in English.
  • Applications usually open between January and April each year. Apply through your home country's dispatching authority (often the Chinese embassy) and complete the online application at campuschina.org or csc.edu.cn. Do not pay any agent that promises a guaranteed place — the official application is free.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a Chinese phone number?
For most things on this page, no. DeepSeek and Qwen accept email or Google sign-in; Kimi's international site accepts Google; the Chinese Text Project needs no account at all. Only Kimi's mainland site and some Chinese platforms ask for a mainland phone number or WeChat — use the international version instead.

Are these really free, or is there a catch?
The chat and learning tools above are free for personal use, usually with a daily quota or a paid tier for heavier use. The scholarship is competitive — it is free if you win it, not guaranteed. Anything on this page that asks for payment before you can even try it is not the official site; go back and check the address.

Can I use these for homework and essays?
Yes for learning and drafting, but check your university's rules on AI use first. Never submit AI output as your own work without checking, and never upload exam questions or other people's private data to a free chat.

Where do I start?
If you only have ten minutes: open DeepSeek or Qwen and ask your hardest study question. If you want to learn Chinese: install HelloChinese and read one text a day on ctext.org. If you want to study in China: check the CSC application dates on campuschina.org now, because the window is only a few months a year.
