Microsoft 365 Education: Free Word, Excel and PowerPoint for Students

Microsoft 365 Education (Office 365 Education A1) is Microsoft's free version for students at partner schools: after verifying with a school email, you can use the web versions of Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OneNote and Outlook, plus Teams, for free for as long as you're enrolled — no credit card and no paid subscription needed.

One common misunderstanding, first: the free A1 plan is web-only. It only works in a browser — you can't download and install the desktop versions of Word, Excel or PowerPoint. Desktop apps only come with the A3 or A5 plans your school buys separately, so check which version your school provides before spending any money.

For most students the web versions are enough: essays, homework and group slides can all be written in the browser, with real-time collaboration and files saved automatically to OneDrive, so a broken computer won't cost you your assignment.

Eligibility

  • Current student or teacher at a school in Microsoft's education program
  • A school-issued email address (.edu or your school's own domain)
  • Free while you're enrolled — your school takes the account back after you graduate or leave

Step 1: Check Whether Your School Already Gave You an Account

Many schools issue a Microsoft 365 account automatically when you enroll, so you may not need to apply at all. Go to https://www.microsoft365.com and sign in with your school email and the password your school gave you. If you can see Word, you already have it — just use it.

Step 2: If You Don't Have an Account, Apply on the Education Page

Open https://www.microsoft.com/education/products/office, click "Get started for students", enter your school email and confirm the verification email. If your school's domain is already on Microsoft's list, activation takes a few minutes; if your school hasn't gone through verification yet, a school admin needs to submit the domain first — that can go through on the spot, or take anywhere from a few days to a month.

What If Your School Doesn't Qualify

There are three fallbacks: first, anyone can use the free Microsoft 365 web apps (Word, Excel and PowerPoint web versions + 5GB OneDrive) with a personal Microsoft account — no school email needed; second, college students can get Microsoft 365 Personal at 50% off, with desktop apps and 1TB of storage, by registering with a personal email and verifying student status with a school email — a payment method is required; third, the 30-day trial requires a payment method and auto-charges when it ends, so it's not recommended just to write one essay.

Before you graduate, back up all your essays and files from OneDrive — once the account is taken back, you can't get them back. The web versions are fully good enough for writing essays, so don't pay extra just for the desktop apps.

FAQ

What's the difference between the free and paid versions?
A1 is web apps and cloud storage only; A3/A5 are paid plans your school buys, adding desktop installs, offline use and advanced security features. A1 is enough for homework and slides.

Is OneDrive 1TB or 100GB?
Most schools still give 1TB, but since June 2026 Microsoft changed the default for new education accounts to 100GB. Go by what your school assigns — just check OneDrive after signing in.

Can I get it without a school email?
Not the education plan. But the free web apps with a personal Microsoft account need no verification — Word, Excel and PowerPoint web versions work the same, just with only 5GB of OneDrive.

Does it conflict with Google Workspace?
No. Use whichever one your school provides — you can get both for free, and their file formats are compatible with each other.
