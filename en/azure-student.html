Azure for Students: $100 Credit for 12 Months, No Credit Card

Azure for Students is Microsoft's free cloud perk for full-time university students: $100 of credit within 12 months, no credit card needed at sign-up, plus 25+ free services. It's plenty for hosting a small website, deploying a course project, or practicing machine learning — and you can renew once a year while you're still enrolled.

Three things to be clear about first: first, when the credit runs out or the 12 months are up, the subscription is disabled — it won't charge you automatically; second, no credit card is needed to claim it, but you must be a full-time university student aged 18 or older; third, remember to turn off services you're not using before the credit runs out — an idle virtual machine burns credit fastest.

Common uses: a small virtual machine running 24/7 to host a personal site or course portfolio will last the full 12 months; or deploy a web app on App Service with a free database. Credit is charged by usage, so check the pricing before creating a service — don't start with a large machine size.

Eligibility

  • Aged 18 or older, enrolled full-time at an accredited two-year or four-year university
  • Verify your student status with a school email — verification is handled by Microsoft's partner, SheerID
  • One Microsoft account can claim the student subscription only once

Step 1: Open the Azure for Students Page

Open https://azure.microsoft.com/free/students and sign in with your Microsoft account (if you don't have one, register first — an Outlook or Hotmail account works). Click apply and follow the prompts to fill in your name, country, school, and date of birth, and enter your school email.

Step 2: Verify Your Student Status

If your school email is on Microsoft's list, you'll get a verification email — click to confirm and you're usually approved within minutes. If your school email isn't on the list, there are two other routes: upload official documents such as an enrollment letter or student ID for SheerID manual verification, which usually takes under 48 hours; or claim the GitHub Student Pack first and activate Azure for Students with your Student Pack status, without submitting documents again. If neither route works, there's also Azure for Students Starter: no verification, no credit card, with a limited set of free services including App Service, Functions, and SQL Database — just no $100 credit. Good for practicing first.

Step 3: Check Your Credit in the Azure Portal

After verification, open https://portal.azure.com — in Subscriptions or the Education hub you can see your student subscription, your $100 credit, and its expiry date. Create a resource group first and put everything you're experimenting with in the same group; when you're done, deleting the whole group is the cleanest way to make sure nothing is left running and charging.

Expiry and Renewal

When the credit runs out or the 12 months end, the subscription is disabled and your services stop — it doesn't automatically switch to paid billing. If you're still enrolled, re-verify once a year to renew. If you want to keep using it, within 90 days of being disabled you can contact Azure support to upgrade to pay-as-you-go — but that's regular billing after the upgrade, so confirm the prices first.

FAQ

What if I don't have a school email?
You can upload an enrollment letter or student ID for SheerID manual verification, or claim the GitHub Student Pack first and then activate. If you have neither, use the Students Starter plan to practice.

What can I spend the credit on?
Most Azure products: virtual machines, web hosting, databases, storage, AI services, and more. Check each service's free allowance and pricing before you start.

Can I renew after graduation?
No. You can't renew after graduation — export your important data and code before it expires, and move your site to another free host.

Does it conflict with the GitHub Student Pack?
No. The Student Pack also includes Azure credit — both channels give you the same kind of student subscription, so don't claim it twice. Claiming the Student Pack first and then activating Azure is the easiest route.
